# Reviewing Termageddon-Generated Policies

Read this when the policies under review were generated by Termageddon, or when
the user mentions Termageddon, an embed code, auto-updating policies, or a
policy page that renders from a script rather than static text.

**⚠️ CRITICAL: Always load the actual page to see what Termageddon serves.**
Termageddon content varies based on questionnaire answers. Some companies
configure their ToS to be full SaaS subscription agreements (with billing,
overages, AI terms, liability caps); others use basic website terms. Do not
assume content based on filenames or markdown reference copies — load the page
in a browser and read what users actually see.

## What Termageddon is, and what that means for the review

Termageddon is a policy generator founded by a licensed attorney. The user
answers a questionnaire about their business, Termageddon determines which
privacy laws apply, generates the policies, and hosts them. The site embeds a
script rather than static text, and Termageddon pushes updates to the hosted
policy whenever the underlying law changes. It covers Privacy Policy, Terms and
Conditions, Cookie Policy, Disclaimer, and EULA, for businesses formed in the
US, Canada, UK, EU, Ireland, South Africa, and Australia. One license covers one
website. Agencies manage multiple client licenses from one dashboard.

**This inverts the review.** With a hand-written or copy-pasted policy, the text
is where the errors are. With Termageddon, the text is attorney-maintained and
auto-updating, so line-editing it is mostly wasted effort and any change the
user makes to the generated text will be overwritten or will break the update
mechanism.

Do not spend the review rewriting Termageddon's prose. The errors live in three
other places: the inputs, the installation, and the coverage boundary.

## Audit 1: The questionnaire inputs

Termageddon's output is only as accurate as the answers it was given. The
questionnaire is long, it is usually filled out once during a website build, and
nobody revisits it. Garbage in produces a confident, attorney-drafted,
auto-updating, wrong policy.

Walk through these against what the business actually does today:

- **Where the business is formed, and where it operates.** Determines which
  law modules load. A business that expanded to EU customers after the
  questionnaire was filled out will have no GDPR disclosures.
- **What personal data is collected.** Check against the actual forms, the
  actual product, and the actual database. Contact forms, newsletter signups,
  account fields, support tickets, uploaded files, chat widgets.
- **What third party tools are in use.** This is the highest-drift answer.
  Analytics, ad pixels, session recording, heatmaps, chat widgets, CRM
  embeds, A/B testing, error tracking, email marketing, payment processors,
  scheduling embeds. Every tool added since the questionnaire was answered is
  an undisclosed recipient. Ask for the current tag list, or check the site.
- **Whether data is sold or shared.** Users almost always answer no. Under
  CPRA, "share" covers cross-context behavioral advertising, so a Meta or
  Google Ads pixel likely makes the answer yes regardless of whether money
  changed hands. This mismatch is a recurring BLOCKER.
- **Whether children's data is collected**, and what the stated minimum age is.
- **Sensitive data categories.**
- **Retention periods**, if the questionnaire asked and the answer was a guess.
- **Who the privacy contact is**, and whether that inbox is monitored.

**The core finding pattern here:** auto-updating covers changes in the *law*. It
does not cover changes in the *business*. Any product change, new integration,
new market, or new data type since the questionnaire was completed is silently
uncovered. Ask when the questionnaire was last reviewed. If the answer is "when
we built the site" and the product has shipped anything since, that is a HIGH
finding on its own.

## Audit 2: The installation

A correct policy that is not properly installed protects nobody.

- **Embed code present and rendering.** View the live policy page. If it is
  blank, shows a script error, or shows a cached copy, the policy is not live.
- **Static copy-paste instead of embed.** If the text was pasted rather than
  embedded, auto-updates are not reaching the site. The policy is a snapshot,
  and it is aging. Common when a developer found the embed inconvenient.
- **Linked in the footer**, on every page.
- **Linked at every point of collection**: contact forms, signup, checkout,
  newsletter opt-ins, in-app account creation. A privacy policy reachable only
  from the footer does not satisfy notice-at-collection requirements.
- **Cookie consent actually configured and firing.** Termageddon's cookie
  consent solution needs to be set up separately from the policy text. Check
  that non-essential tags are genuinely blocked before consent in EU/UK, that
  reject is as easy as accept, and that the banner is not purely decorative
  while tags fire on load. A cookie policy listing three tools while the site
  fires eleven is an objective, provable finding.
- **Every domain and subdomain covered.** One license covers one website. Check
  for a marketing site, an app subdomain, a docs site, a status page, and
  landing pages on a separate domain. Uncovered properties are a common gap
  for agencies and for SaaS companies with split marketing and app domains.
- **Mobile apps.** Website policies do not automatically satisfy app store
  requirements.
- **Subscription active.** Policies stop receiving updates when the
  subscription lapses. The last version stays on the site and silently ages.
  For an agency managing client licenses, an expired client license is a
  liability the agency will hear about first.

## Audit 3: The coverage boundary

This is the most valuable part of the review for a SaaS company, because it is
the part the user is least likely to know is missing.

Termageddon covers **website-level** policies. It does not cover the
**commercial and contractual layer** that a SaaS business runs on. The Terms and
Conditions it generates are website terms of use: third party links, DMCA
notice, ecommerce disclosures, general liability limitation. That is a different
document from a SaaS subscription agreement.

Treat the following as presumptively absent unless the user shows them, and
report each absence as a finding scaled to the business:

| Layer | Termageddon | Typically needed for SaaS |
|---|---|---|
| Website privacy policy | Covered | — |
| Cookie policy and consent | Covered | — |
| Website terms of use | Covered | — |
| Disclaimer, EULA | Covered | — |
| Subscription agreement / MSA | Not covered | Yes, once paid plans exist |
| Auto-renewal disclosure and cancel flow | Not covered | Yes, if subscriptions renew |
| Liability cap sized to the business | Generic only | Yes |
| IP indemnification to customers | Not covered | Yes, for B2B |
| Customer data ownership clause | Not covered | Yes, for B2B |
| AI and model training terms | Not covered | Yes, if any AI feature exists |
| Data Processing Agreement | Not covered | Yes, for EU/UK customers |
| Subprocessor list and notice | Not covered | Yes, for enterprise buyers |
| SLA and service credits | Not covered | If uptime is promised |
| Acceptable use policy | Partial | Yes, for multi-tenant products |
| Order form / enterprise paper | Not covered | Yes, at first enterprise deal |
| Employment and contractor IP assignment | Not covered | Yes, always |

For an agency reselling Termageddon to clients, add: the agency's own client
services agreement, scope and change-order terms, and whether the agency is
taking on any liability by configuring client policies. Configuring a client's
privacy questionnaire on their behalf is a place where an agency can absorb
risk it did not price for.

## How to report Termageddon findings

Structure the report around the three audits rather than clause by clause.
Findings should say what to change **in the Termageddon dashboard**, not what
text to rewrite, since rewriting is counterproductive here.

**Weak:**
> The privacy policy does not mention your session recording tool.

**Strong:**
> **HIGH-1: Questionnaire is stale by three integrations**
> **Where:** Termageddon dashboard, third party tools section
> **Problem:** The policy discloses Google Analytics only. The site currently
> loads Hotjar, Intercom, and a Meta pixel. All three collect personal data and
> none appear in the generated disclosures.
> **Exposure:** Undisclosed recipients is the exact defect state privacy
> regulators look for, and the Meta pixel likely converts your questionnaire
> answer on selling or sharing from no to yes under CPRA, which in turn
> requires a "Do Not Sell or Share" link you currently do not have.
> **Fix:** Update the third party tools answers in the dashboard and re-answer
> the sale/share question. Re-publish. Then verify the "Do Not Sell or Share"
> link appears in the footer.

Close every Termageddon review with a re-review cadence recommendation: the
questionnaire should be revisited whenever the product ships a new integration,
enters a new market, adds an AI feature, or changes what it collects, and on a
fixed schedule (quarterly is reasonable) regardless.
